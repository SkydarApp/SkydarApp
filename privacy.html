Skydar Privacy Policy

Effective October 22, 2026

In short: Skydar has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no tracking. To show what's in your sky, it sends your approximate location to a few data services listed below. We don't run any servers and we never see or sell your data.

This policy explains what information Skydar (the iPhone app and its Lock Screen Live Activities, together "the app") uses and why. The app is published by Cameron Brenneise ("we", "us").

Information stored on your device

To work, the app saves the following on your iPhone:

  • Your settings, such as alert preferences, Live Activity choices and your sky's light-pollution level.
  • Your last known location, so the app can open quickly and plan pass alerts in the background.
  • Downloaded data such as satellite orbits, comet and asteroid positions, and recent flight routes, so the app works with a poor connection.

This information stays on your iPhone. Deleting the app deletes it.

Location

If you allow it, the app uses your location while it is open to work out where planes, satellites, planets and stars appear in your sky. To fetch data for your area, it sends your location to these services:

  • adsb.lol, to find aircraft near you. Your location is rounded to about 100 meters.
  • Apple WeatherKit, for the stargazing forecast. Apple's handling of these requests is covered by Apple's Privacy Policy.
  • NASA JPL, to find which comets and asteroids are visible from your area. Your location is rounded to about 1 kilometer.

Location is not sent to us, not linked to your identity, and not used for tracking. You can turn location access off at any time in the iPhone Settings app; Skydar then can't place objects in your sky.

Other data services

The app also downloads data that doesn't involve your location:

  • adsbdb: when you tap a plane, its flight callsign (for example "UAL123") is sent to look up its route.
  • CelesTrak: satellite orbits.
  • NASA JPL Horizons and Close-Approach Data: positions of comets, asteroids and spacecraft.
  • NASA Deep Space Network: live antenna activity.

As with any internet request, these services can see your device's IP address. Their own privacy policies apply to how they handle it.

Camera and motion

The camera shows the live sky behind the labels, and motion sensors tell the app which way your iPhone is pointing. Both are processed on your iPhone in real time. Camera video is never recorded, saved or sent anywhere.

Notifications and Live Activities

Pass alerts are scheduled on your iPhone from data already on it. Lock Screen and Dynamic Island countdowns get their information directly from the app. None of this passes through our servers.

Skydar Pro subscriptions

Subscriptions are sold and managed by Apple through the App Store. We do not receive your name, email, payment details or Apple Account information. The app only checks with Apple whether your subscription is active. You can manage or cancel your subscription in your Apple Account settings.

What we don't do

  • We don't require an account or ask for your name or email.
  • We don't use analytics, advertising, or third-party tracking tools.
  • We don't track you across other apps or websites.
  • We don't sell, rent or share your information.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we will update this page and the effective date above. Significant changes will also be noted in the app's release notes.
